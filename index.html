About

I study the legal architectures that govern automated systems — what law assumes about machines, and what machines reveal about law.

My work sits at the intersection of administrative law, algorithmic accountability, and the theory of technology regulation. I am interested in how legal categories built for human actors strain and break when applied to automated decision-making.

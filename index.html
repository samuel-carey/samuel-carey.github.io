About

I am a PhD candidate at the Swedish Law and Informatics Research Institute (IRI) at the Department of Law, Stockholm University. I am affiliated with the interdisciplinary research environment WASP-HS and the cluster The Rule of AI — AI, Regulation and Society.

My doctoral project is a temporal and empirical investigation of technological change in the legal profession. Using qualitative methods — including semi-structured interviews and ethnographic fieldwork — I examine how lawyers encounter, resist, and adapt to new technologies, and how the profession mobilises to regulate and govern that change through evolving institutional and governance forms.

My broader research concerns the regulation of AI risks. I am interested in how specific epistemic communities — expert groups working at the intersection of technology and policy — produce authoritative accounts of risk, and how those accounts travel into legislative and regulatory frameworks. Working within the socio-legal tradition, and drawing from critical systems theory, STS, and political economy, I examine the relationship between how norms around the risks of technology form, the power of those that develop them, and their hardening within the public sphere.
